# 2. Contribution Score and Its Presentation

## 2.1 The Score

Peers aggregate the receipts they have *received* (as uploader) into a **Swarm Contribution Score** $\Theta_A$. A verifying node computes it from a set of receipts $R$ as

$$\Theta_A = \sum_{j \in R} w_j \cdot \mathbb{I}\bigl(\text{VerifySignature}(PoU_j)\bigr) \cdot \text{Bytes}(PoU_j) \cdot \varphi\bigl(\text{Age}(PoU_j)\bigr)$$

where:
*   $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ verifies that the signature matches the S/Kademlia identity of the issuing downloader and that the receipt names $A$ as uploader and $K_s$ as the stream;
*   $\text{Bytes}(PoU_j) = 16\text{ KB} \times \text{popcount}(\text{BlockBitmap}_j)$ — the verified payload the receipt attests;
*   $w_j = 3$ for a `RELAYED` receipt (Ch6 §6.3.2), otherwise $1$; **`PULL` receipts from any one counterparty are credited at most $\beta_{\text{pull}} \times 1\text{ s} = 62.5$ KB per segment**, so that two peers exchanging fake repair traffic cannot pump each other faster than genuine repair could;
*   $\varphi(t) = e^{-\lambda t}$, $\lambda = 0.005\ \text{s}^{-1}$, devalues old receipts (a receipt older than 5 minutes is worth $< 25\%$), forcing peers to maintain active, continuous contribution.

The **delivered-throughput rate** that gates multi-tree standing (Ch1 §1.2.1) is the undecayed 60-second sum, with age measured in **segments** against the verifier's own live edge $X_{\text{edge}}$ (Ch4 §4.3.1) rather than by the receipt's downloader-signed timestamp, which a colluding downloader could post-date:

$$\Theta^{\text{rate}}_A = \frac{1}{60\text{ s}} \sum_{j : X_{\text{edge}} - 60 \le \text{SegmentSeq}(PoU_j) \le X_{\text{edge}}} w_j \cdot \min\bigl(\text{Bytes}(PoU_j),\ \text{Cap}(PoU_j)\bigr) \cdot 8 \quad [\text{bit/s}]$$

where the **per-receipt cap** is what one segment of the named tree can honestly contain: $\text{Cap} = 1.5 \times \text{BitrateKbps}_m \times 1\text{ s}$ for a tree receipt (the declared per-tree bitrate from the slicing matrix, with $50\%$ headroom for encoder variance — $\approx 140$ KB on a $0.75$ Mbps stripe, $280$ KB on $1.5$ Mbps) and $\beta_{\text{pull}} \times 1\text{ s} = 62.5$ KB for a `PULL` receipt. The **rank** used in tree admission (Ch1 §1.2.2) is $\Theta^{\text{rate}}_A / B$ — full-stream-equivalents currently being delivered. A leaf, or a node that has relayed nothing yet, has rank $0$.

## 2.2 Presenting the Score: `RANK_PROOF`

"$A$ presents a bundle of verified receipts" is the mechanism by which rank enters a decision at another node — a tree parent deciding admission, or a peer weighing $A$'s accusation (§5.3.3). It cannot mean sending every receipt: a super node's 60-second window holds $\approx 520{,}000$ of them. Nor can a verifier trust a self-reported total — not of bytes, and not of receipts. The presentation is a **non-interactive commit-then-sample proof**, `RANK_PROOF` (0x19, Appendix D §D.4.17), whose sample establishes *three* things about the committed list: that its members are genuine, that they are **distinct**, and how many bytes a typical member carries.

1.  **Commit.** $A$ takes its receipts from the trailing 60 segments, keeps at most one per $(\text{SegmentSeq}, \text{TreeID}, \text{RxFlags}, \text{Downloader})$, sorts them by that **canonical key** (strictly increasing, fields compared in that order, NodeIDs big-endian), builds a Merkle tree over the leaf hashes $\text{Blake3}(\text{receipt body})$ padded with zero leaves to a power of two, and signs $(\text{ReceiptCount}, \text{CommitSegmentSeq}, \text{ReceiptListRoot})$ during segment `CommitSegmentSeq`.
2.  **Nonce.** The sample is selected by a value $A$ could not have known when it committed: the Merkle root of a chunk `MANIFEST` whose `SegmentSeq` is **greater than** `CommitSegmentSeq` — signed by the source after the commitment existed. Every peer holds recent manifests, so the verifier checks the nonce against its own copy (or fetches it with `MANIFEST_REQUEST` if it is within $\tau_{\text{retain}}$).
3.  **Sample.** $p_i = \text{Blake3}(\text{ReceiptListRoot} \parallel \text{Nonce} \parallel i) \bmod \lceil \text{ReceiptCount}/2 \rceil$ for $i = 0 \ldots 7$ selects eight **adjacent pairs** $(2p_i, 2p_i + 1)$. $A$ attaches both receipts of each pair — sixteen receipts — with the Merkle path from each pair's parent node to the root. When `ReceiptCount` is odd the last pair's right leaf is the zero leaf.
4.  **Verify.** The verifier checks $A$'s signature; that the nonce manifest post-dates the commitment and is one it holds; that each sampled receipt verifies (downloader signature, uploader $= A$, stream $= K_s$, `SegmentSeq` within the last 60 segments of the verifier's live edge); that each pair's leaf hashes and path reach `ReceiptListRoot`; and that within each pair $\text{key}(\text{left}) < \text{key}(\text{right})$ **strictly**. It then computes
    $$\Theta^{\text{rate}}_A \approx \frac{\text{ReceiptCount}}{60\text{ s}} \cdot \overline{w \cdot \min(\text{Bytes}, \text{Cap})}_{\text{16 samples}} \cdot 8$$
    — the count the list was committed to, times the mean capped byte value of the receipts actually inspected. **Any** failed check rejects the whole proof, and a peer presenting a proof that fails is treated as having presented a forged signature (Ch7 §7.1.1: local ban).

**Why pairs, and why no `TotalBytes`.** An earlier version signed a prover-declared `TotalBytes`, bounded only by $\text{ReceiptCount} \times 8$ MB, and sampled single leaves at arbitrary positions. Every sampled receipt could be genuine while the two declared numbers were fiction: an honest home relay with 600 real receipts ($57.6$ MB, rank $1.28$) could declare $4.8$ GB and pass with rank $107$, or list each real receipt a thousand times, declare $600{,}000$ leaves, and pass with rank $1{,}280$ — sixteen random draws over 600 distinct receipts collide with probability under $20\%$, and Merkle paths for $2^{20}$ leaves fit in the frame. Sorting by a canonical key and checking the two leaves of each sampled pair in strict order is what makes `ReceiptCount` a count of *distinct* receipts: a duplicated leaf must sit next to its original in sorted order, so a fraction $f$ of duplicated leaves puts at least a fraction $f$ of adjacent pairs in violation, and the check fails with the same probability as fabrication does. A hash-ordered list would *not* have caught this — sorted duplicates preserve every order statistic — which is why the key is the receipt's content, not its hash. Reading bytes from the sampled receipts themselves, each capped at what one segment of the named tree can honestly contain, removes the second free variable.

Because the ordering is fixed before the nonce exists, $A$ cannot grind the list to steer the sample away from fabricated or duplicated entries. Inflating the list by a fraction $f$ of receipts that are fake, duplicated, or outside the window survives the sample with probability $(1-f)^8$: $10\%$ at $f = 0.25$, $0.4\%$ at $f = 0.5$. The byte estimate has sampling error of its own — sixteen receipts from a mix of $0.75$ and $1.5$ Mbps stripes estimate the mean within about $\pm 15\%$ at one standard deviation — which is why rank thresholds in this specification are ratios with margin ($1.25\times$ for preemption, $0.25$ for accuser standing) rather than exact values; a prover cannot exploit the error, since it does not choose the sample. Chapter 8 owes the measurement of whether eight pairs or sixteen give the false-reject and false-accept rates the admission rule needs. A proof is $\approx 8 \times (2 \times 250 + 32 \cdot (\lceil \log_2 \text{ReceiptCount} \rceil - 1))$ bytes — under $10$ KB for a super node, $5$–$6$ KB for a home relay with a few hundred receipts — and is sent once per admission attempt on the QUIC control stream, or once per accusation (§5.3.3), never per block.

A parent that has held $A$ as a **child** for a while needs no proof to rank $A$ as a *downloader*; but it learns nothing about $A$'s contribution *to others* except through `RANK_PROOF`. That is why admission reads the proof and not the parent's local view.

**What the proof does and does not establish.** It proves that `ReceiptCount` distinct receipts, each signed by a distinct-keyed downloader naming $A$ as uploader, exist in a list $A$ committed to before the nonce was known, and it estimates their bytes. It does **not** prove that those downloaders are distinct *people*: a downloader identity costs $\approx 10$ ms (Ch2 §2.2.1), and a downloader that is the uploader's own Sybil signs for blocks that were never sent. The protocol's bound on that is address diversity (§5.3.2), which a verifier can apply only to signers whose address it can resolve — relay-class signers through the DHT, leaf-class signers not at all. Rank is therefore **a claim the uploader can forge at a cost of identities, not of bandwidth**, and every consumer of rank is bounded independently of the claim's honesty: the prefix cap on child slots at honest parents, the reliability term on multi-tree standing, and the evidence requirement on accusations (§5.3.2 sets these out). The proof's job is to make *honest* rank comparable and *lazy* forgery — declaring a number — impossible; it is not, and cannot be, a Sybil defence.
