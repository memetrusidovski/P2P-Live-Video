# Trace: super-node-obligations

Walker scenario `super-node-obligations`. One 10 Gbps relay at the largest fan-out the
specification permits, walked through one segment (1,000 ms, four 250 ms chunks). Every
per-child, per-block and per-second obligation is multiplied out from Appendix B constants and
checked for cores, bytes and asymptotic order. Where the spec gives a range, the worst end is
walked too.

## 4. Candidate findings

### F1 (High) The XDP allowlist is keyed by IPv4 source address but peers are connections: children behind one CGNAT address share one 2,000 pps bucket, and the first of them to disconnect deletes the entry for all
- Rests on: Ch7 §7.2.1 "User space completes the QUIC handshake and validates the peer's S/Kademlia identity. On success it **inserts** `(source IP → {rate, burst})` into a kernel-resident map; on disconnect or eviction it **removes** the entry." Ch7 §7.2.2 listing: `__type(key, __u32); /* peer IPv4 source address */`. Ch7 §7.2.1 itself: "a carrier-grade NAT address is shared by thousands of viewers". App B `RATE_PPS_PEER` 2,000, `RATE_PPS_UNKNOWN` 50.
- With inputs: a relay with n children behind one address (the prefix cap admits up to ceil(K_v / 20) = 261–500 per /24 per tree at a super node, all of which may be one /32 under CGNAT); each child sends its parent ~108 pps of QUIC ACKs at ack-every-2 on a 216 pps push (22 pps with ACK-frequency tuning) plus 1 receipt, ~0.5 PULL and <= 4 probes per second.
- The spec produces: one bucket of 2,000 pps shared by n children; at n >= 18 (108 pps) or n >= 90 (22 pps) ACKs are dropped in the NIC driver for **every** child behind the address, QUIC loss detection on the parent-to-child path declares loss, the congestion controller throttles the datagram push and those children starve on clean links; receipts and probes in the same bucket are dropped too, so the receipt deadline (Ch5 §5.2.1) evicts them after 3 segments. Independently of n: when one child behind the address disconnects, user space removes the entry and the remaining n-1 fall to tier 2 at 50 pps shared, burst 100 — below one child's ACK rate — until each re-handshakes.
- Wrong because: the allowlist is the mechanism R11 relies on to serve CGNAT populations, and the per-address budget was sized for the **unknown** tier (SOLUTION-037) while the allowlisted tier still assumes one peer per address; SOLUTION-017's residual risk does not mention shared addresses. Bites at any relay with two children behind one IP (the delete bug) and at any relay with ~20+ (the rate bug); worst at exactly the super nodes the shield exists to protect. Chapter 8 §8.3 Scenario C puts 75 % of peers behind symmetric NAT or CGNAT.
- Fix sketch: key the allowlist by (saddr, sport) — XDP already parses the UDP header, and a NAT allocates a distinct external port per child flow; or keep the IP key with a user-space refcount and a per-entry rate set to n x RATE_PPS_PEER; size the inbound rate for the parent direction (ACKs, receipts, probes) rather than the media direction; state in Ch7 §7.2.1 which direction the 2,000 pps protects. Touches Ch7 §7.2.1, §7.2.2 listing, App B B.1.4, App C. IPv6 inherits the same question when its filter is written.
- already_known: false (ISSUE-017/037 closed; neither covers the allowlisted tier under shared addresses).

### F2 (Medium) The PULL unchoker's electorate is the Active Set (<= 64) while PullSlots at a super node is 2,000, so 97 % of the reserve has no allocation rule and the choke state of the 9,936 children outside the Active Set is undefined
- Rests on: Ch5 §5.1.2 "Every 500 ms, the peer executes the following scheduler to evaluate reciprocity across its Active Set A" and "— two slots on a 10 Mbps uploader, twenty on 100 Mbps, **two thousand on 10 Gbps**". Ch3 §3.1.1 "The c_a = 8 cap sizes the *control plane* (gossip, bitfield exchange, TFT state)" and c_a^eff = min(64, ...). Ch4 §4.3.3 line 11: a peer pulls from `GetNeighborsWithBlock(ActiveSet, ...)` — its own Active Set, which contains its tree parents.
- With inputs: u_v = 10 Gbps -> PullSlots = floor(1,000 / 0.5) = 2,000; |A| = 64; 10,000 [5,217] children each holding S in their own A.
- The spec produces: lines 14–19 unchoke NeighborsList[0..1,995], i.e. all 64 members unconditionally (Threshold_min cannot bind); lines 21–25 iterate an empty range; **1,936 slots (968 Mbps) are never assigned**; the 9,936 children not in A receive no CHOKE_STATE and PULL from S with a state the spec never sets. Reading (a), default CHOKED: a super node's children get no repair from the one peer that is certain to hold their blocks, while 968 Mbps of its reserve idles; their FEC-failure demand is 37–71 % of that reserve at rho = 0.02. Reading (b), default UNCHOKED: draw is bounded only by beta_pull per child = 9,936 x 0.5 = 4,968 Mbps against a 1,000 Mbps reserve. The reference implementation chose a third reading (serve tree peers regardless, ISSUE-066).
- Wrong because: the spec's own slot figure for a 10 Gbps node cannot be realised by the loop it defines, and two implementers diverge on a state that decides whether a child can repair at all. Distinct from ISSUE-066 (which is about reciprocity and cooldown for children *inside* the mechanism): here the children are outside the electorate altogether.
- Fix sketch: define the unchoker's population as "every peer with an open session that may PULL from this node" or state the default choke state for peers outside A; bound children's repair draw against the reserve explicitly (e.g. tree children served up to r_pull u_v shared, PullSlots computed over the remainder); reconcile "two thousand on 10 Gbps" with c_a^eff = 64. Touches Ch5 §5.1.2, §5.1.3, Ch3 §3.1.1, Ch4 §4.3.3; cluster with ISSUE-066.
- already_known: false (adjacent to ISSUE-066).

### F3 (Low) Source pacing over half the chunk period leaves >= 125 ms of silence per chunk on every under-filled link, so the heartbeat fires every chunk and the child's probe races it — contradicting the stated zero cost while media flows
- Rests on: Ch3 §3.3.2 "This costs one small packet per idle 100 ms per child and **nothing at all while media is flowing**" and "a child at any depth sees a near-continuous trickle of symbols with gaps far shorter than tau_ping"; App B source pacing "<= 1/2 chunk period", tau_ping = 100 ms.
- With inputs: a 250 ms chunk whose symbols leave over <= 125 ms; tau_ping = 100 ms; a relay below ~55 % utilization (or the source itself, or a chunk with 2 blocks on a 1.5 Mbps tree).
- The spec produces: a gap of 250 - 125 = 125 ms (112 ms after one 13 ms block time) > 100 ms on every chunk -> a heartbeat per chunk per child = 4/s per child; the child's own probe fires at silence = 100 ms, the instant the heartbeat arrives, so under any jitter both fire: up to PING + PONG + heartbeat = 12 control packets per child per second instead of 0. At a saturated relay (94 % utilization, 3 blocks per chunk) the serialization spread closes the gap to 16–92 ms and nothing fires; at a 10 Gbps node the cost is therefore 0–40,000 heartbeats/s (0–2.6 MB/s) depending on fill, and at the source it is 4/s per child always.
- Wrong because: both quoted claims are false by the spec's own constants; the cost is small (0.1–0.5 % of egress) but the detector's soundness argument ("gaps far shorter than tau_ping") does not hold, and the probe path becomes a steady per-chunk load rather than an exception. Two sibling traces (parent-loss-repair S10, media-pipeline-latency §silences) assumed it harmless; it is, in the spec's frame form — in the implementation's form it is F5.
- Fix sketch: either pace over the full chunk period (adds <= 125 ms to glass-to-glass, still 4.1–4.3 s), or set the child's probe at tau_ping + margin so the heartbeat wins the race, or state the per-chunk heartbeat as the normal cost and correct the two sentences. Touches Ch3 §3.3.2, App B tau_ping / pacing rows.
- already_known: false.

### F4 (High, impl) `on_receipt` scans the whole receipt ledger three times per receipt, so receipt handling is O(k x ledger) — 1.8 x 10^10 element visits per second at a super node
- Rests on: `src/crates/bs-core/src/node/incentive.rs` `on_receipt`: `self.incentive.receipts.iter().find(|r| r.pou.canonical_key() == key)` then `self.incentive.receipts.retain(|r| r.pou.canonical_key() != key)` then `self.incentive.receipts.retain(|r| r.pou.segment.0 >= lo)`, over `receipts: Vec<StoredReceipt>`; `theta_rate()` and `theta_decayed()` also iterate the Vec.
- With inputs: 10,000 receipts/s [5,217] and a 60-segment ledger of 600,000 [313,000] entries.
- The implementation produces: 3 x 600,000 x 10,000 = 1.8 x 10^10 key constructions and comparisons per second (each `canonical_key()` builds a 38-byte tuple) — tens of core-seconds per second, versus the ~0.5 core the spec budgets for the Ed25519 work itself. At K_v = 10 it is 18,000 visits/s and invisible, which is why the simulator did not see it.
- Wrong because: the spec's receipt path is O(k) and "under one core"; the implementation's is O(k^2 x 60) and cannot run a super node.
- Fix sketch: index the ledger by canonical key (`BTreeMap<CanonicalKey, StoredReceipt>`), keep a per-segment bucket so the window trim is one `split_off` per segment, maintain Theta^rate incrementally.
- already_known: false.

### F5 (Medium, impl) Heartbeats and liveness probes are plain-UDP `PING`s carrying a signed 152 B validation block, verified on receipt and answered with a signed `PONG`, where the spec puts the heartbeat on the QUIC connection with no validation block
- Rests on: Ch3 §3.3.1 "a heartbeat (a QUIC `PING` frame, or the protocol `PING` on the control stream)"; App D §D.2: QUIC control frames carry "No validation block repeated". Impl `node.rs` `liveness()` -> `send_ping(addr)` -> `validation_block(FrameType::PING, ...)` on `Channel::Udp` for both the child-idle heartbeat and the parent probe; `on_ping` runs `verify_vb` (PoW checks + Ed25519 verify) and replies with a signed `PONG`; `on_pong` verifies again.
- With inputs: F3's idle gaps on every under-filled link; k = 10,000 children.
- The implementation produces: per heartbeat one Ed25519 sign (~20 us) at the parent, one verify (~60 us) and one sign at the child, one verify at the parent for the PONG — ~160 us of asymmetric crypto and 188 + 163 B of UDP per 100 ms of idle per child. When a gap crosses 100 ms on every link at once (a 2-block chunk, an upstream hiccup, or a node below ~55 % fill) that is 10,000 signs + 10,000 verifies in one burst at S, i.e. up to 40,000 + 40,000 per second = **~1 + ~2.5 core-seconds per second**, exceeding the receipt path the spec sizes a core for; the spec's form costs no signatures.
- already_known: false.

### F6 (Low, impl) Bitfield gossip goes to every tree child, not to the scaled Active Set
- Rests on: Ch3 §3.1.1 — the relay-broadcast bitfield to all K_v children is "deliberately not required by this spec" and "re-creates the O(K_v) per-parent cost that the scaled Active Set exists to avoid"; impl `pull.rs` `active_peers()` = tree parents ∪ children ∪ membership.active, used by `gossip_bitfields()` every tau_gossip for the last 3 chunks.
- Numbers: 10,000 x 3 frames x ~75 B = 2.25 MB/s = 18 Mbps egress and ~30,000 inbound bitfield frames/s from children (each child gossips to its parents) at k = 10,000, versus 19 KB/s in the spec's form. O(k) rather than O(1); 0.1 % of egress, so cost is minor — the divergence is the point.
- already_known: false.

## 6. Spec silences assumed through

- **S1** When a prover commits its receipt list for `RANK_PROOF` (every segment, or on demand). Assumed on demand; a per-segment rebuild at a super node is ~0.4 core-s.
- **S2** How many chunks a `BUFFER_STATE_BITFIELD` round covers and whether one frame per chunk is required (the `BlockIndex` layout forces per-chunk frames for contiguous bitmaps). Assumed 4 recent chunks, one frame each.
- **S3** The default choke state of a peer that has an open session but is not in the node's Active Set (F2).
- **S4** Which children a fallen K_v(m) drains (incentive-timeline S3; the impl drains the newest).
- **S5** Whether "parity is computed per block, per downstream link" means a per-link *count* or a per-link *encode*. Assumed count: RaptorQ symbols are deterministic in (block, ESI) (Ch4 §4.1.2), so one encode at E_max serves every child.
- **S6** Whether `BLOCK_PROOF`, `MANIFEST` and per-packet headers count as `Bytes_control` in the CDO KPI. Walked both ways (1.85 % / 0.7 %).
- **S7** The 16-bit ceiling on `K_avail` (PROBE_RESPONSE, ROSTER, PROOF_OF_UPLOAD) and `ChildCount` (ROSTER): 65,535 per tree, i.e. ~63 Gbps single-tree on a 0.75 Mbps stripe or ~113 Gbps at 1.5 Mbps. Fine at 10 Gbps.
- **S8** The heartbeat's frame form (QUIC PING frame vs protocol PING on the control stream) and size. Assumed ~65 B on the wire.
- **S9** The serialization order of a block across k children (round-robin per block assumed; the spec names none), which decides which child is "last" (ISSUE-072).
- **S10** How a super node resolves 10,000 downloader keys per second for receipt verification — ISSUE-065 (open); assumed cached from the session handshake.
